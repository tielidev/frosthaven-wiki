## What is this build trying to do?

Take dependable attacks, practical movement and sustain before investing in elaborate element-dependent chains. Make a recovery plan at the same time as the attack plan. Keep track of the next round's health exposure, not just the current hit.

This is a role-based strategy framework. It does not claim to be a verified level-by-level digital card, equipment or perk list. Check the current digital card text before making an irreversible selection.

## How do you set up a repeatable turn?

1. Check the health cost of the intended attack and recovery sequence.
2. Engage where support is available and avoid adding unnecessary enemy attacks.
3. Recover only when the resulting hand and health state produce a safe following round.

Health is a tactical budget rather than an invitation to absorb every attack. Spend it where recovery or offense creates a clear advantage, and agree with the party when healing is needed. Keep a route to the next objective after a melee exchange.

## Which cards should you prioritize?

Build around a repeatable attack-and-recovery rhythm, with movement that still works when no ideal element is available. One with the Mountain is digital-confirmed as a persistent effect with regeneration-related fixes. Cold Boulder and Shattering Blow are confirmed options to inspect for the supporting hand, while Bring Down the Mountain is explicitly classified as melee in recent notes. Plan the recovered card and health state together.

Compare a candidate card against the job your current hand cannot accomplish: reaching a useful hex, making the primary attack legal, surviving the next enemy action or recovering a disrupted turn. Avoid upgrading a number while losing the only action that keeps the sequence reliable. Exact level selections remain subject to digital Codex verification.

## How should equipment and perks support the plan?

Prioritize sustain that helps fund the recovery cycle rather than armor selected solely for the biggest health number. Use movement equipment to avoid spending health absorbing attacks during an otherwise empty travel turn. Inspect recovery item timing relative to your persistent effect.

Read the digital wording of Sure Footing and Chilling Blow before pairing them with movement or Gift of the Mountain. Prioritize a benefit that keeps the usual recovery loop stable over a payoff that needs several conditions simultaneously.

## What should you change when the plan fails?

A long rest is not simply another trigger for the usual recovery engine: historical official notes state that One with the Mountain does not trigger while long resting. Check the current interface and choose a rest window that does not leave the hero exposed.

Identify whether the failure was positioning, timing, a resource shortage or a party mismatch before replacing the entire hand. Record a change and test it over another expedition rather than changing several decisions at once.
