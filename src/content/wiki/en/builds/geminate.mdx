## What is this build trying to do?

Keep a functional blend of attacks, movement and form-changing options across both forms. Spend a loss when it advances the objective or resolves a dangerous engagement, then rebalance future choices around the reduced hand.

This is a role-based strategy framework. It does not claim to be a verified level-by-level digital card, equipment or perk list. Check the current digital card text before making an irreversible selection.

## How do you set up a repeatable turn?

1. Count the currently usable cards in both forms.
2. Choose the next form based on reachable targets and remaining movement.
3. Execute a form transition that leaves a complete pair available for the following round.

Plan the current turn and the form you will need on the following turn. Count usable cards in each form before resting. The goal is to leave a coherent sequence available, with movement that makes the next form's attacks practical.

## Which cards should you prioritize?

Keep both forms equipped for the next cycle: a legal attack at the required distance, movement to reach that distance and a way to leave the form when needed. Hornbeetle Carapace, Reshape the Guise, Dragonfly Surge and Scarab Flight are digital-confirmed cards. Treat their element and defensive effects as specialized tools, and do not replace every flexible card with a conditional attack from your preferred form.

Compare a candidate card against the job your current hand cannot accomplish: reaching a useful hex, making the primary attack legal, surviving the next enemy action or recovering a disrupted turn. Avoid upgrading a number while losing the only action that keeps the sequence reliable. Exact level selections remain subject to digital Codex verification.

## How should equipment and perks support the plan?

Movement tools are especially useful when they preserve a precise range band or the next form's access. Do not assume an item that adds ordinary range changes a printed precise-range restriction. Check each digital preview before spending resources on that combination.

Acquired Resistance is mentioned in recent digital notes, which explicitly say it cannot prevent quest and event effects. Evaluate it for the effects it actually covers. An ordinary damage improvement may be preferable when you are already managing two forms and do not want another conditional trigger.

## What should you change when the plan fails?

A defense card used only for movement can be productive; an element-consuming action canceled without performing its ability is not free engine progress. Check that the intended action resolves before counting its form or element consequences.

Identify whether the failure was positioning, timing, a resource shortage or a party mismatch before replacing the entire hand. Record a change and test it over another expedition rather than changing several decisions at once.
