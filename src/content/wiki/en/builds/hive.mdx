## What is this build trying to do?

Start with a small number of useful summons and a mode that complements them. Keep transfer decisions tied to safety and target access. Prefer an engine you can explain clearly over several simultaneous conditional interactions.

This is a role-based strategy framework. It does not claim to be a verified level-by-level digital card, equipment or perk list. Check the current digital card text before making an irreversible selection.

## How do you set up a repeatable turn?

1. Select an initial mode for the immediate engagement.
2. Place useful summons with future transfer positions in mind.
3. Before transferring, inspect the destination and the enemy turns that will occur there.

Think of the board as a network of useful summon positions. Choose a mode for the engagement you are actually facing, then protect the units that give you useful transfer options. Reassess the network whenever the party crosses a doorway.

## Which cards should you prioritize?

Choose a primary mode and a small summon group with different useful positions. Aimed Assault, Rapid Fire and Code Geminate are all named together in digital interaction fixes, so inspect them as candidates when considering a ranged attack package. Reassemble and Disassemble provide further confirmed card names to review; preserve a plan for rebuilding after a unit is lost rather than filling the hand entirely with initial deployment.

Compare a candidate card against the job your current hand cannot accomplish: reaching a useful hex, making the primary attack legal, surviving the next enemy action or recovering a disrupted turn. Avoid upgrading a number while losing the only action that keeps the sequence reliable. Exact level selections remain subject to digital Codex verification.

## How should equipment and perks support the plan?

Inspect whether an item can be used by the character, modifies a summon or merely becomes relevant after Transfer. Circlet of Eyes has a documented interaction with Aimed Assault and Rapid Fire, making it a candidate to review for a multi-target plan when available. It is not proof of an unlimited attack chain.

Overcome is digital-confirmed in condition and Transfer fixes; Adaptive Programming and Regroup also appear in official notes. Choose by the failure you encounter most: condition exposure, transfer flexibility or rebuilding. Verify current point costs rather than copying historical corrected values.

## What should you change when the plan fails?

Transfer can move the character into a condition or attack situation previously borne by a summon. Do not use a mode card as though the character were immune to all conditions applied to its units; read the current transfer handling.

Identify whether the failure was positioning, timing, a resource shortage or a party mismatch before replacing the entire hand. Record a change and test it over another expedition rather than changing several decisions at once.
