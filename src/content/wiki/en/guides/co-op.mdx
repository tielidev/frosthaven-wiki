## What should the party discuss?

Name the dangerous enemy, the objective to advance and the positions that must stay clear. Tell teammates if your turn depends on an ally being nearby, an element being available or a target surviving. Discuss a contingency for the hero most likely to open a room or become exposed. Follow your selected ruleset's restrictions on sharing hidden card information.

## How do you avoid planning gridlock?

Assign outcomes rather than scripting every click. One hero can handle an exposed attacker while another prepares the doorway, with a third keeping a support option available. After enemy actions reveal, update only the decisions that changed. Let the active player finish confirmations clearly before another player triggers an interaction that depends on them. If somebody must step away, agree how the party will pause or reconnect rather than assuming every action can be undone safely.

## What should you do when a session misbehaves?

The 1.0 notes include networking improvements, but no release can promise every session is faultless. Record the scenario, turn order and action that preceded an unexpected result. Check whether the problem is an objective rule or a card interaction before calling it desynchronization. Avoid repeatedly advancing the session while clients display different states. Use the game's supported recovery flow, and include concise reproduction information in any report. After the session, adjust one recurring coordination problem rather than blaming an entire class or player.

## Which references should you keep nearby?

Use the [class library](/classes/) to understand your party, consult [card economy](/mechanics/card-economy/) when planning endurance, and open the [world map](/maps/) between expeditions.
