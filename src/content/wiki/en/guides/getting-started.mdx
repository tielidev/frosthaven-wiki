## What should you do before the first mission?

Use the tutorial to learn what the interface asks you to confirm, then inspect each hero's available actions. Choose a small set of repeatable combinations rather than trying to memorize an entire class. The official new-player material recommends Story difficulty for beginners; lowering difficulty is a sensible learning tool when several systems are new at once. Keep the scenario objective visible. Clearing enemies, protecting a figure and reaching a location can demand very different decisions.

## How should you plan each round?

First identify the enemy or objective that can make the next round worse. Next check whether your hero can reach a legal position to contribute. Select actions with a fallback if that enemy dies before your turn. After enemy actions reveal, inspect movement and attack ranges before confirming your own movement. Spend a strong effect when it prevents a dangerous turn or completes an objective; preserve it when ordinary actions already solve the immediate problem.

## What should you change after a defeat?

Review the first round where the plan became expensive. Did the party open a room before everybody could act, spend losses without removing danger, or chase loot while an objective deteriorated? Fix that specific decision before changing the whole party. A retry is more useful when you bring a different opening sequence and a reason for it. Keep a brief note of the cards or movement tools you repeatedly wished you had.

## Which references should you keep nearby?

Use the [class library](/classes/) to understand your party, consult [card economy](/mechanics/card-economy/) when planning endurance, and open the [world map](/maps/) between expeditions.
