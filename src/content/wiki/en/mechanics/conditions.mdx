## How do you choose a condition target?

Read the enemy's revealed action first. A movement-related condition matters differently for a distant melee enemy than for one already in attack range. An attack-preventing effect is most valuable on a dangerous attacker that would otherwise survive. Conditions should solve the threat in front of you rather than decorate an enemy that the party is already certain to defeat.

## How should you handle conditions on allies?

Inspect what the condition prevents and when it ends. Decide whether to remove it, play a useful unaffected action or reposition to make the next round safer. Check the digital tooltip rather than relying on a remembered Gloomhaven interaction. Some heroes deliberately interact with negative conditions, but that does not mean every condition or every timing window helps them. Coordinate before applying a cost or accepting transferred harm.

## What are the frequent mistakes?

Do not assume that a boss accepts every condition; inspect its immunities and scenario rules. Do not build a turn around a status effect that expires before the intended follow-up. Healing, condition removal and attack prevention can have different outcomes, so read the ability rather than treating them as interchangeable. When a combination seems exceptionally strong, verify each step separately: legal target, application, duration and the effect on the actual enemy action. Revise the plan if any step fails.
