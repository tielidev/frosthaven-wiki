## When is acting early useful?

Early action is valuable when you can remove or disable a dangerous enemy before it acts, move away from a threatened position or secure an objective in time. It does not automatically make a turn safe. Moving toward several enemies early can simply give all of them convenient attacks. Compare the effect you will achieve with the attacks that your final position permits.

## When can acting late help?

A later turn can let approaching enemies spend movement without reaching you. You may then move in and attack after their turns. This is a planning technique rather than a guaranteed rule: ranged attacks, unusually large movement and special scenario behavior can defeat the expectation. Check the revealed enemy actions and keep another option. If you end next to a survivor, plan whether the following hand can act early enough to leave or finish the fight.

## What are the common failures?

Players sometimes choose a late turn because they remember an enemy was slow last round. Its next action can be different. Others use a late approach but forget that their remaining cards cannot provide a quick following turn. Treat initiative as a sequence across rounds, not a single number. Discuss who needs the first attack and who is waiting for a doorway to clear. Avoid sharing exact hidden information when your chosen ruleset restricts it.
