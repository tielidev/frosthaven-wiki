## Why does card conservation matter?

The number of turns you can continue acting depends on your available cards, not only your current health. A loss action can be excellent, but spending one early reduces what you can recover on later cycles. Evaluate the entire encounter: a burst that removes an enemy before several attacks can save resources, while a flashy attack into a nearly defeated target may buy nothing.

## How should you prepare for a rest?

Inspect which cards are in hand, discard, active and lost before the next round. Identify the engine piece you cannot afford to lose and check how your chosen rest works in the digital interface. If the room is under control, coordinate a safe rest window rather than leaving one hero alone to absorb the next wave. A long rest may help recover appropriate spent equipment, but it also changes that round's contribution.

## How do you avoid running out near the objective?

Do not automatically spend a loss on every opening hand. Estimate how much travelling and fighting remains, and keep movement available for the final stretch. When the party repeatedly exhausts shortly before victory, compare unnecessary setup cards and avoidable defensive losses before reducing damage output indiscriminately. If a scenario has a clock, excessive conservation is also a mistake: use powerful effects to shorten the dangerous section. Check class-specific recovery and scenario exceptions rather than applying one universal stamina formula.
